I'm Daniel Brockwell, a portfolio manager in digital assets at Nickel Digital Asset Management. I help run an eight-figure, long-only liquid fund: I find investment and trading ideas, make the case for them, execute them and manage the positions and yield strategies that follow. I'm certified under the FCA Certification Regime for client dealing.

I got here through computer science. I studied it at the University of Sheffield, where my dissertation was an Android app that used machine learning to categorise spending automatically from Open Banking transaction data. During my year in industry I supported the internal systems at the Intellectual Property Office, and I joined Nickel as a DeFi Analyst in January 2022, moving into portfolio management that November.

The engineering is still a large part of the job. I run the fund's own Ethereum node, wrote the on-chain alerts that watch for depegs, large withdrawals and lending-rate spikes, simulate transactions before they are sent, backtest other managers' strategies, and build and run market-making bots. Two moments I'm glad of: a [profitable trade around the Ethereum Merge](/work/ethereum-merge/) in 2022, built on Aave's stale exchange rates on the proof-of-work fork, and an alert in April 2024 on falling liquidity in Synthetix's sETH wrapper that flagged sUSD depeg risk early, after which the fund exited its sUSD exposure.

Outside work I like small, exact programs and long computations. I build games and tools for Android and for the Psion Organiser II, where OPL and 6303 machine code, a few kilobytes of RAM and a screen of two or four lines mean every byte and every millisecond has to be argued for. On the PC I search for large primes and compute mathematical constants: I found the third-largest known twin primes and hold the world record for log(10).

The same habit runs through all of it: understand the system, measure it, then build the tool.
